Wave Motion

The transfer of energy through a medium by vibration of molecules is called wave motion.

Transverse Waves

Particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. Examples include light waves and ripples on water.

Longitudinal Waves

Particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation. Sound waves traveling through air are a primary example.

Real-World Optics & Physics

Prisms & Optical Geometry

When light waves enter an equilateral prism under specific angles, they undergo total internal reflection. The light wave is reflected totally from the second surface and emerges perpendicular from the third side, perfectly demonstrating geometric wave behavior.

Seismology

Earthquakes produce two primary types of wave motion: P-waves (longitudinal compressions) and S-waves (transverse shears). Because S-waves cannot travel through liquids, they prove that the Earth's outer core is liquid.

Electromagnetic Waves

💡 Did you know?

"Rogue waves" were considered sailors' myths until 1995. We now know they are real phenomena caused by extreme constructive interference—when multiple smaller ocean waves perfectly align their crests to suddenly form an unpredictable wall of water up to 100 feet tall!

Test Your Understanding

Challenge 1: Kinematics of a Wave
Question: A sound wave travels through air at 343 m/s. If its frequency is 440 Hz (the musical note A), what is its wavelength?

Answer: The wave equation is $v = f \lambda$. Rearranging for wavelength gives $\lambda = \frac{v}{f}$.
$\lambda = \frac{343}{440} \approx 0.78$ meters.
Challenge 2: The Stadium Wave
Question: When sports fans do "The Wave" in a stadium, is it a transverse or longitudinal wave?

Answer: It is a transverse wave. The "energy" of the wave moves horizontally around the stadium, but the medium (the people) move vertically (up and down), which is perpendicular to the direction of travel.
